Pretorsion theories and image-closed subcategories

The intersections of the two components of pretorsion theories are exactly the image-closed subcategories, and the pretorsion theories form a complete lattice. For the linearly oriented quiver of type A, pretorsion theories and image-closed subcategories are counted by Genocchi and median Genocchi numbers, refined by the q-Gandhi polynomials of Han and Zeng. All of this is checked in Lean.

Let Λ be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field k. A pretorsion theory (𝒯, ℱ) in mod Λ generalizes a torsion pair: morphisms from 𝒯 to ℱ factor through 𝒵 = 𝒯 ∩ ℱ, and every module sits in a sequence 𝒯 ∋ T → X → F ∈ ℱ that is exact up to morphisms factoring through 𝒵. A subcategory is image-closed if it contains the image of every morphism between its objects.

Theorem A. For a subcategory 𝒵 of mod Λ, the following are equivalent: 𝒵 is image-closed; 𝒵 = Gen 𝒵 ∩ Cogen 𝒵; 𝒵 = 𝒯 ∩ ℱ for a pretorsion theory (𝒯, ℱ).
Theorem 3.6. The pretorsion theories of mod Λ, ordered by (𝒯, ℱ) ≤ (𝒯′, ℱ′) iff 𝒯 ⊆ 𝒯′ and ℱ ⊇ ℱ′, form a complete lattice.
Theorem B. For the linearly oriented quiver 1 → 2 → ⋯ → n, counting pretorsion theories by the number of indecomposables in 𝒯 ∩ ℱ gives Bn+1(1, q), and counting image-closed subcategories by their number of indecomposables gives Cn+1(1, q), the q-Gandhi polynomials of Han and Zeng.

Counting in type A

Let Λn be the path algebra of 1 → 2 → ⋯ → n. Theorem B counts the pretorsion theories PT(Λn) by the number of indecomposables in 𝒯 ∩ ℱ, and the image-closed subcategories Lim(Λn) by their number of indecomposables:

∑(𝒯, ℱ) ∈ PT(Λn) q|ind(𝒯 ∩ ℱ)| = Bn+1(1, q),    ∑𝒵 ∈ Lim(Λn) q|ind 𝒵| = Cn+1(1, q).

The q-Gandhi polynomials are given by B1 = C1 = 1, Bm = Δq(x2Bm−1) and Cm = (1 + qx) Δq(xCm−1), where Δqf(x) = (f(1 + qx) − f(x)) / (1 + (q − 1)x). At q = 1 the counts are the positive Genocchi numbers g2n+4 and the median Genocchi numbers H2n+3 (OEIS A110501 and A005439).

For n = 2, B3(1, q) = q3 + 4q2 + 7q + 5 and C3(1, q) = q3 + 3q2 + 3q + 1. The proof is combinatorial: image-closed subcategories of mod Λn correspond to families of intervals in {1, …, n} closed under nonempty intersections (Theorem 5.5); quotient-closed and submodule-closed subcategories are recorded by cutoff vectors, and Theorem 6.5 decides, vertex by vertex, which pairs form a pretorsion theory; adjoining one vertex at a time gives the recurrences of B and C, with a second variable x counting the indecomposables whose support ends at n (Theorems 7.5 and 8.5).

What Lean checks: the identities in ℤ[q] and their two-variable refinements, for every n. The table, the evaluation at q = 1 and the identification with Genocchi numbers (classical results of Han and Zeng) are not formalized; the table was recomputed from the recurrences.

Context

Pretorsion theories were introduced by Facchini and Finocchiaro for categories without a zero object, and developed for general categories by Facchini, Finocchiaro and Gran. In mod Λ, Campanini, Fedele and Yıldırım showed that the first and second components of pretorsion theories are exactly the quotient-closed and submodule-closed subcategories, and Campanini and Fedele constructed pretorsion theories whose intersection is a prescribed Serre subcategory. Theorem A determines all possible intersections. It parallels a result of Enomoto and Sakai: the subcategories closed under images and extensions are the intersections of a torsion class and a torsion-free class.

The q-refinement follows the quotient–submodule equidistribution conjecture of Enomoto, which counts subcategories by their number of indecomposables. The counts link module categories of type A with Genocchi numbers and the q-Gandhi polynomials. Pilaud also obtained the count H2n+3 for intersection-closed interval families. The proofs use recurrences; the paper knows no bijection with the Genocchi permutation models of Han and Zeng that preserves the statistics. The counts depend on the orientation of the quiver, and only the linear orientation is treated.

The results concern two groups of readers: those working on the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras, in particular torsion theory and lattices of subcategories, and those working in enumerative combinatorics on Genocchi numbers and Gandhi polynomials.

The paper

The paper Pretorsion theories and image-closed subcategories by Haruhisa Enomoto characterizes the intersections of pretorsion theories, describes the lattice of pretorsion theories and the realizations of an image-closed subcategory, studies image closure as a convex geometry on indecomposable modules, and counts pretorsion theories and image-closed subcategories for a linearly oriented quiver of type A. This repository proves every numbered statement of the paper and the cited inputs it uses. The correspondence record maps each numbered statement to its Lean declarations.
